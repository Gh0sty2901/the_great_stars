Iron Secretary - Tyrant - Genocide

# Short Story: 

September 1, 2020, 11:20 UTC. Artillery shells hammered the ground above the bunker, and gunfire rippled somewhere beyond it. Each impact shook the walls and sifted dust onto the map at the center of the table. Inside the command center of the Communist League Central Committee, voices stacked on voices.

General Josef slammed his hands down at his end of the table. "The Elbe offensive has failed. We are stuck. The Republic's bombers keep pounding our supply lines, and the front is starving for bullets and food. Worse yet, revisionists in our ranks are wreaking havoc on our logistics and our unity."

"Then stop looking east, General." Colonel Friedrich leaned forward and tapped the Rhineland on the map. "The Rhine holds the industrial capacity we need, and the separatists there have next to no materiel. Move our artillery west, take the Rhine, and supply the front. Berlin can wait."

Brigadier Georg sat silent, watching each face around the table. He noted who would turn on whom first, and how easily.

Let them wrangle. Before the first shell fell that morning, he had sent word to the 10th Red Infantry Battalion: open talks with the National Syndicalists of the eastern league. Offer them a place in the National Proletarian Front, and a reason to abandon their own leadership.

The argument climbed until it filled the bunker. Georg, who had not spoken once, stood. He did not raise his voice, and he did not touch the table.

"Gentlemen. We are spread too thin, and the Elbe is bleeding us." He let the dust settle on the map. "Withdraw from the river. Let the eastern league advance into the gap. Once they overreach their supply, we close the pocket and finish them."

The room went quiet for the first time.

"It's Mao's own logic," said a young cadre near the door, a little too quickly. "Lose land but keep men, and the land can be retaken." His eyes flicked to Georg and away.

Josef said nothing at first. Dust drifted down onto the map and settled over the Elbe. "If the league breaks through at Magdeburg, we lose the corridor," he said at last.

"Then we make sure they don't get that far," said Georg.

Josef looked at him a moment longer than necessary. Then he nodded once. They had no real alternative, and everyone at the table knew it.

Josef picked up the transceiver. His jaw worked before he spoke.

"All units. Withdraw from the river."

The reply crackled back almost at once. "Withdraw? Sir, we have them on their heels."

"I SAID WITHDRAW. DO AS YOU'RE TOLD."

The line went dead. Across the table, Georg folded his hands and looked down at the map, where the dust had settled over the Elbe like ash..

Josef turned his glare on him. "If this fails, Brigadier, you'll answer for it. Remember who gave the order, and who proposed it."

Georg did not look up. "Of course, General."

Beyond the bunker walls, the shells kept falling.

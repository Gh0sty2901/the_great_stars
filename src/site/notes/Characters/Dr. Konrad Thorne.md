The sun prophet

# Short Story: 

The Heimdall core hummed, a low, constant roar that filled the cramped control room. Dr. Konrad Thorne stood among the blinking lights and drifting printouts, eyes locked on the terminals. His favorite clipboard, brown leather worn smooth at the edges, rested in the crook of his arm. He scrawled across it without looking down: numbers, theories, half-formed ideas, all of it spilling past the ruled lines in the shaky hand of a man who hadn't slept. Too many nights, too much coffee, too long in Sector 0.

A young scientist edged toward him. "Uh, sir, I think you should rest for a while. Some of us on the team have unanimously voted that you take at least a week off." He hesitated. "To clarify by 'some of us,' I mean everyone at the Citadel, including the security detail."

"Nonsense, dear boy. I'm perfectly fine." Konrad drained his fifth coffee of the day and sat the mug down. "They gave me command of this place, and I will run it personally, no matter what." The pen kept moving.

"Sir, this isn't up for debate. The petition is already with the central committee. One way or another, you're taking that week."

Konrad scoffed, "Who will keep the citadel running while I'm gone then huh? Who will make sure this place stays exactly on course."

"We will, sir." A pause. "Please."

For a moment Konrad's grip tightened on the clipboard. He glared around the room at every face, each one steady, none of them backing down. Then his shoulders dropped. He pressed the clipboard into the scientist's hands and strode for the door.

"DO NOT TOUCH MY BELONGINGS!" he shouted as the blast doors closed behind him.
